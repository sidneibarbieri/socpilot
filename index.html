Plans, Not Prompts: Verifying Policy Compliance for LLM‑Assisted Incident Response

Sidnei Barbieri · Leonardo Vaz de Meneses · Ágney Lopes Roth Ferraz · Wagner Comin Sonaglio · Lourenço Alves Pereira Júnior

Computer Science Division, Aeronautics Institute of Technology (ITA), Brazil

Overview

Security operations centers increasingly use large language models as copilots that propose incident-response plans for analyst review. Existing evaluations assess instruction adherence, generated text, or downstream tool execution; none of them establishes whether the plan presented to an analyst satisfies the organization's declared policy.

Compliance becomes decidable when the representation changes, not when the model does: a plan projected onto a finite action alphabet admits an exact decision procedure, where free text admits only judgment.

SOCpilot projects a model's free-form proposal onto a canonical action trace over a finite action catalog — an operation we call operational policy projection — and decides compliance there, with a deterministic, typed verifier, after generation and before any execution. A subjective judgment over prose becomes a reproducible decision over a plan: the same trace yields the same verdict regardless of which model emitted it.

What the evaluation object provides

  • A frozen corpus of real incidents from a production financial-sector SOC, canonicalized and anonymized under a dual-review release protocol, with a reserved export batch for prespecified transfer checks.
  • A reviewed, typed operational policy (mandatory, ordering, restraint, and approval rules) derived through a documented selection protocol, with every retained rule traceable to data, decision, and test.
  • A deterministic verifier with typed, auditable repairs, whose determinism, provider-independence, and idempotence are enforced by released checks rather than assumed.
  • Provider-free public replay: every reported number regenerates from frozen evidence without calling any model.
